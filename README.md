## A data science project to report the findings derived from observing crime-based data
### This project explores patterns of ethnicity, stop and search, prosecutions, and convictions in England and Wales using three open government datasets from data.gov.uk. It was submitted as part of the Data Science Programming module of the MSc Data Science programme at the University of Birmingham.
###The analysis combines census population data (2021), magistrates court prosecution and conviction records (2009–2017), and national stop and search records (2006–2023) to investigate whether criminal justice outcomes are distributed proportionally across ethnic groups — and where they are not, what might explain the gap.
### Key questions explored include: How have prosecution and conviction rates changed over time? Which offence types are most likely to result in conviction? How disproportionate is stop and search activity by ethnicity, and has that disproportionality improved or worsened over time? And how efficiently do stop and searches translate into prosecutions across different ethnic groups?
### The project finds that while White groups dominate in absolute counts — consistent with their 81.7% population share — Black individuals are stopped at 4 to 9.5 times the rate of White individuals per 1,000 population, a gap that worsened after 2014 despite falling overall stop and search volumes. Drug and public order offences carry conviction rates above 90% once charged, while sexual offences sit at around 52%. Males account for 6.2 times more prosecutions than females — a gap larger than any ethnic difference observed in the data.
## Tools: Python (pandas, numpy, matplotlib, seaborn) · Jupyter Notebook · data.gov.uk open datasets
